Under the hood

How a chat with Claude on a Mac turned into 715 illustrated scenes, Genesis, 1 and 2 Samuel, 1 and 2 Kings, the four Gospels and the Acts of the Apostles, rendered on a box on my desk. Everything here is in the GitHub repo, including the raw numbers.

    Why the numbers differ

    One cast for the Gospels

    Matthew, Mark, Luke and Acts reused John's portraits, so the New Testament shares one cast. Each Old Testament book brought its own.

    Mark: a busy box

    Mark ran while a separate workflow had three AI coding agents working on the same machine. Its median edit took 92 seconds instead of 57.

    Luke and Matthew: quiet again

    On a quiet, freshly rebooted box, Luke's per-job times came back almost to John's, the clearest sign the agents slowed Mark. Matthew matched Luke almost to the tenth of a second.

    Zooming in costs attempts

    From Mark on, every image was checked region by region, which catches small things a full-frame look misses. That is most of why later books took more tries than John.

    Acts: crowds and furniture

    Same per-job times as Luke and Matthew, but more attempts: more crowds, Roman rooms full of modern furniture, and six new faces to keep straight.

    Genesis: the easiest

    Run overnight with no one watching, and the fewest attempts of any book. Much of it is sky, sea and animals, and every prompt started from the lessons of the books before it.

    Samuel: aging and reviews

    David had to age across four portraits, the ark needed its own reference and a rule that nobody touches it, and three rounds of Brian's review caught what the first pass let through.

    Kings: chariots and castles

    1 Kings fought glass jars, ceiling lights and a model that cannot draw a chariot. In 2 Kings every distant city came out as a medieval castle or a modern town.

    Housekeeping on the box

    Before Luke, the Halo was rebooted to clear dozens of orphaned Chromium processes. Those didn't reappear during the Luke run, but a python3 process belonging to ComfyUI stayed resident after ComfyUI was closed properly, holding about 2.4 GB of memory. A full restart is needed to get the machine back to a clean state before the next run. Before Matthew the box was checked again and came back clean: only ComfyUI running, no language model loaded, 0.5 GiB of GPU memory in use.

    The pieces

    Claude calls a tool like halo_generate_image. The MCP server fills in a ComfyUI workflow, queues it, polls until the image is done, saves the PNG on the Mac and hands a preview back into the chat. ComfyUI only listens on the Halo's localhost. The Mac reaches it through an SSH connection that launchd opens on demand, so nothing is exposed on the network. Setup steps are in setup/.

    The MCP tools

    halo_generate_image

    Text to image. Pick a model or take the default. Pass a reference_image to put the same face in a new scene, or a trained lora.

    halo_edit_image

    Plain-language edits with Qwen-Image-Edit. With no path it edits the last image, so fixes chain naturally.

    halo_list_models

    What's installed, each model's tuned defaults, and what it can do.

    halo_train_lora

    Uploads a folder of photos and trains a LoRA with ComfyUI's built-in trainer, with status and cancel tools alongside.
